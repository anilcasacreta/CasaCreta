Before You Start

Pre-start Checks
  1. Confirm the studio and build base meet the requirements below.
  2. Remove everything from the printable area before homing.
  3. Check the air pipes and connectors for correct operation and leakage.
  4. Switch on the compressor and confirm 14–16 bar tank pressure.
  5. Load prepared clay and set the cylinder feed pressure to 10–12 bar.
  6. Home the machine and confirm the nozzle position above the bed.
Studio Environment and Build Base
  • Operate in a ventilated, dry, clean, and level studio area.
  • Recommended studio temperature: 15–35 °C.
  • Recommended relative humidity: 20–80%.
  • Secure the printer on a flat and stable surface.
  • Use a firm, moisture-resistant wooden build board that cannot warp under wet clay.
Warning: A warped or loose build board can distort the first layers and cause the printed piece to fail.
Clay Preparation and Consistency
  1. Pug and de-air the clay thoroughly before loading.
  2. Remove hard particles, plastic, dry fragments, and all foreign material.
  3. Prepare a smooth, uniform paste without air bubbles.
  4. A studio starting reference is approximately 30 parts water to 100 parts dry clay by weight; adjust for the clay body and test extrusion.
  5. The clay must extrude smoothly while remaining stiff enough to hold the printed layers.
Record required: approved clay body, measured water ratio, batch number, and successful settings.
Opening the Clay Cylinder
  1. Release all cylinder pressure using the regulator control.
  2. Confirm the cylinder is fully depressurized.
  3. Remove the air pipe from the top of the cylinder.
  4. Only then open the cylinder cap.
Danger: Never open a connected or pressurized cylinder. Trapped pressure can launch the heavy metal cap without warning, causing a crushing impact, broken bones, or serious injury.
Loading the Clay Cylinder
  1. Open the cylinder only after completing the depressurization procedure above.
  2. Clean the cylinder interior with a soft damp cloth, then confirm it is clean and dry.
  3. Form the prepared clay into small, dense portions.
  4. Pack each portion firmly from the bottom upward, compressing every layer to remove air gaps.
  5. Leave 2–4 cm of free space at the top for plunger movement.
  6. Install the plunger and close the cylinder evenly and securely.
  7. Check the pressure seal before operation.
Do not: Strike the lid or cylinder with a hammer or heavy tool. This can deform the closure and damage the pneumatic pressure seal.
New batch rule: Do not add fresh clay over old clay. Empty and clean the cylinder, then load one consistent fresh batch.
Homing, Manual Movement, and Extrusion

Home the machine

  1. Open Menu → Movement → Home.
  2. Select Home to synchronize all axes with their reference positions.

Move an axis manually

  1. Open Menu → Movement → Move.
  2. Select a movement step of 0.1, 1, or 10 mm.
  3. Move one step at a time. Do not press movement controls repeatedly.

Extrude manually

  1. Open Menu → Movement → Extrude.
  2. Set the step value to 10 and speed to Slow.
  3. Run extrusion while watching the hose outlet or nozzle.

Start a Print

Print Sequence
  1. Prepare the model as STL, OBJ, or 3MF.
  2. Slice it using the approved ClayBot Mega profile.
  3. Keep the toolpath inside the 1000 × 1000 × 1000 mm build volume.
  4. Export the G-code and load it using a USB drive.
  5. Confirm nozzle size, layer height, speed, extrusion rate, and print position.
  6. Set cylinder feed pressure within the recorded 10–12 bar operating range.
  7. Run manual extrusion and purge the older clay from the delivery pipe until fresh clay flows continuously.
  8. Open Print, select the correct file, and select Start.
  9. Watch the first 3–4 layers continuously.
Data required: approved slicer profile, start/end G-code, layer height, auger rate, and origin.
USB / Pen Drive Rule
Danger: Do not remove the USB drive while the print job is running.

During Printing

What to Check
  • Watch the first 3–4 layers continuously.
  • Continuous clay flow
  • Uniform bead width and layer height
  • Good bonding between layers
  • No bubbles, gaps, or nozzle blockage
  • Stable walls without leaning or collapse
  • Free movement of the hose and printhead
  • Never leave the machine running completely unattended.
Flow Rate and Layer Quality
  • Balance print speed with clay flow. Excessive flow at low speed produces thick, irregular deposits.
  • Calibrate Z height so each layer bonds firmly to the layer below.
  • Layers must not rest loosely on one another.
  • Do not squash layers so tightly that clay spreads into a sticky mass.
Live Adjustments and Wavy Extrusion

Speed or flow correction

  1. Open More → Speed.
  2. Use ADD/DEC to adjust speed or flow rate in 1%, 5%, or 10% increments.
  3. Use the smallest correction possible and observe several toolpath lines before changing it again.
Note: Mid-print changes can create visible blotches in the finished piece.

Wavy extrusion

  1. Select Pause.
  2. Stop extrusion and isolate electrical power and compressed air.
  3. Depressurize the clay system before touching the printhead.
  4. Inspect and tighten the fasteners directly above the nozzle.
  5. Restart only after the printhead and pressure system are secure.
What Not to Touch or Change
  • Do not leave the printer completely unattended.
  • Do not reach into the motion area.
  • Do not touch moving parts or the nozzle.
  • Do not open the pressurized cylinder.
  • Do not disconnect pressurized hoses.
  • Do not move the bed or printed piece.
  • Do not make large pressure or setting changes during printing.

Finish the Print

Finish Sequence
  1. Confirm printing, motion, and extrusion have stopped.
  2. Stop the auger.
  3. Park the printhead using the machine controls.
  4. Isolate the compressed-air supply.
  5. Depressurize the clay system and confirm the gauge reads zero.
  6. Remove the piece with its build plate where possible.
  7. Move it on a flat, rigid support to the drying area.
  8. Label and record the print.
Print Record
  • Job/part ID
  • File name and revision
  • Date and operator
  • Clay batch/recipe
  • Nozzle size
  • Layer height and speed
  • Auger setting and air pressure
  • Print result and defects

Cleaning

Cleaning Steps
  1. Stop the machine, isolate electrical power, and fully depressurize the clay system.
  2. Remove remaining clay before it dries.
  3. Clean the nozzle, removable clay-contact parts, build plate, and spills using the procedures below.
  4. Inspect the nozzle, auger, hose, seals, and fittings.
  5. Dry cleaned parts before reassembly or storage.
Data required: approved auger-disassembly and internal printhead-cleaning procedure.
Nozzle Cleaning
  1. Remove the nozzle only after the machine and pressure system are isolated.
  2. Wash the removed nozzle under warm water immediately after printing.
  3. Clean the opening with a wooden skewer or plastic pick.
  4. Confirm no clay remains inside the opening.
  5. Dry the clean nozzle before storage or reinstallation.
Do not: Use metal needles or metal picks. They can scratch or enlarge the nozzle opening and change extrusion accuracy.
Delivery Pipe and Cylinder Cleaning

Delivery pipe

  1. Disconnect and remove the pipe only after complete depressurization.
  2. Flush the detached pipe thoroughly with water away from the printer and electrical equipment.
  3. Remove all clay buildup and dry the pipe before reconnection.

Clay cylinder

  1. Open the cylinder using the approved depressurization procedure.
  2. Wash the interior with water and a soft sponge.
  3. Do not use abrasive metal scrubbers.
  4. Rinse, dry, and inspect the cylinder wall, plunger, and pressure seal before reassembly.
Exterior Cleaning
  • Use only a soft damp cloth or microfiber cloth on non-electrical exterior surfaces.
  • Use a clean, non-shedding cloth; loose fibres can contaminate clay or become caught in moving hardware.
  • Do not spray water or cleaning liquid directly onto the machine.
What Must Never Be Cleaned with Water
  • Touchscreen, controller, switches, or electrical cabinet
  • Motors, drivers, sensors, or connectors
  • Bearings, rails, belts, screws, or lubricated parts
  • Voltage stabilizer or power connections
Purging Old Clay from the Material Pipe
  1. Depressurize the system before disconnecting the pipe from the nozzle.
  2. Secure the pipe outlet inside a collection container.
  3. Confirm there is enough clay in the cylinder.
  4. Apply cylinder pressure and purge the old clay from the 1.5–2 m pipe.
  5. Stop before the cylinder becomes empty.
Danger: Never let the cylinder run empty while purging old clay through the detached material pipe. At 10–12 bar, compressed air can suddenly blast the remaining clay from the pipe outlet and cause serious injury. The hazard in this condition is the violent clay-and-air discharge from the pipe. Secure the outlet inside a collection container, keep everyone away, and stop before the clay runs out.

Troubleshooting

If This Happens → Do This
ProblemMain Action
Clay not extrudingCheck clay supply, pressure, auger, hose, and nozzle.
Blocked nozzleStop, depressurize, remove, and clean the nozzle.
Uneven extrusionPurge air; check clay consistency, pressure, auger rate, and blockage.
Wavy extrusionPause, isolate power and air, depressurize, then inspect and tighten the fasteners above the nozzle.
Air bubblesUse de-aired clay and refill the cylinder without air pockets.
Print collapsingUse stiffer clay or reduce layer height, flow, or unsupported geometry.
Layers separatingCheck clay moisture, layer height, print speed, Z height, and extrusion flow.
Machine stopsRecord the error; check power, file, controller, and axis obstruction.
Nozzle collisionStop immediately; check homing, origin, bed level, and nozzle offset.
Hose or fitting leakStop, isolate air, depressurize, and replace the damaged part.
File rejectedRe-export G-code using the approved slicer profile and USB drive.

Emergency / Do Not Do

When to Stop Immediately
  • Person or object in the motion path
  • Nozzle or axis collision
  • Hose, fitting, regulator, or cylinder leak
  • Abnormal noise, vibration, heat, smell, or smoke
  • Uncontrolled extrusion
  • Print collapse into the printhead path
Emergency Actions
  1. Press the emergency stop if fitted and identified. If no emergency stop is available, switch off power at the main control when safe to do so.
  2. Keep people away from moving and pressurized components.
  3. Isolate electrical power and compressed air when safe.
  4. Do not open the clay system until the gauge reads zero.
  5. Do not restart until the fault is identified.
Data required: emergency-stop location, reset procedure, main isolator location, and Casa Creta emergency contact.
